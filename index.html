About, methods & data terms

Methods & accuracy
  • Sun, Moon, planets, twilight, rise/set and Moon phases are worked out in your browser with Astronomy Engine 2.1.19 (MIT, Don Cross). The repo's tests check them against JPL Horizons and timeanddate.com:
    • positions are within 0.4′
    • rise, set, twilight and Moon-phase times are within ±1 min
    • Moon illumination is within 1 percentage point
    Rise and set assume a flat, sea-level horizon. Real hills and buildings change them.
  • Twilight: the Sun at −6° is civil, −12° nautical and −18° astronomical. "Dark" means the Sun is below −18°. Polar day and polar night are shown as "doesn't happen".
  • Stars: 1,600+ stars down to magnitude 5, with constellation lines from d3-celestial (BSD-3-Clause, Olaf Frohn; Hipparcos/HYG). They are converted from J2000 to the current date with precession and nutation, and refraction is applied.
  • Satellites: SGP4 (satellite.js 5, MIT) runs on CelesTrak "visual" elements in a Web Worker. A pass counts if it rises at least 10° above the horizon. It is marked visible when the satellite is sunlit (cylindrical Earth-shadow model) while your Sun is below −6°. Brightness isn't predicted. Accuracy gets worse as the elements age, so their age is shown.
  • Observing score: "SKYHUD heuristic v1" is our own design, not a published standard. It combines six factors:
    • clouds 0.45 (low 1.0, mid 0.8, high 0.5)
    • Moon 0.20 (illumination × altitude)
    • darkness 0.15
    • light pollution 0.10 (the Bortle class you pick)
    • transparency 0.05 (visibility and humidity)
    • seeing/comfort 0.05 (wind and dew-point spread)
    Total cloud of 90% or more caps the score at 10. It does not forecast astronomical seeing. Open "Why this score?" to see every input.
  • Meteor showers: peaks come from IMO λ☉ (J2000), with the Moon's illumination worked out for each peak. Events are hand-checked and each one cites its source. Items we couldn't verify are left out.
Data sources & terms

Privacy

Your location is saved only in this browser's localStorage, rounded to 0.01° (about 1 km). That rounded copy is sent to Open-Meteo to fetch your forecast, and is in the lightpollutionmap.info link (sent only if you click that link); otherwise it stays in this browser. Use “Forget my location” in the location dialog to delete it and the cached forecast. Open-Meteo keeps request logs for up to 90 days. A place search sends only the name you type. Satellite, space-weather and ISS requests don't include your location. Pass predictions are worked out on your device.
